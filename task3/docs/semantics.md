# Calculations for the 2025 export

**Stage results.** One row represents one bib finishing one stage. Adjusted seconds equal finish seconds minus bonus seconds plus penalty seconds. Rankings and winners for a single stage follow the exported finish position. Stages 1, 17 and 21 contain differences between position order and recorded times. A signed gap compares raw seconds with the rider recorded first. The export does not identify a cause for those differences. On stage 13 two riders share position 108 and the next position is 110.

**General classification (GC).** At each checkpoint, rank riders who completed every stage so far and have yet to exit. Lower cumulative adjusted time wins. Equal times share a competition rank, matching the database views. A rider's rank refers to the full eligible field, including when viewing an individual team. **Young Rider** uses the same calculation for riders born on or after 1 January 2000 in the 2025 race. The separate under-26-on-stage-1 comparison uses exact age on that day.

**Points and Mountains.** The supplied database has no rider-level sprint/finish or climb scores. Page 03 now supplements it with the embedded `Jersey Leaders` table: published first-place rider and cumulative score for each of the 21 stages. The final winners are Jonathan Milan (372 points) and Tadej Pogačar (119 mountain points). The stage selector uses its existing latest-selected-stage rule; clearing it shows stage 21. Published tie-break ordering determines the leader where points are equal. These snapshots do not supply full-field rider rankings or individual scoring events. [Sources](jersey-sources.md). The alternative mountain-time ranking compares eligible riders' cumulative adjusted time on mountain stages. The GC progression mountain series marks total race time at mountain checkpoints.

**Best Team.** For each team and stage, add the three fastest adjusted times. Their riders can change each day. A team needs three finishers to score a stage. Sort by scored stages descending and total top-three time ascending. The deficit chart compares teams with the leader's stage coverage. Page 01's rider-hours chart sums all completed efforts, including rides before a withdrawal.

**Participation.** Use rider nationality for the roster, wins, podiums and best final GC rank. Count registered teams by team nationality. One missing rider country, bib 78, appears as Unknown in the counts and roster. The map uses known country names. Speed divides total rider-distance by total raw finish hours.

**Exits and timing scenarios.** DNS and mid-stage withdrawal retain the recorded exit stage. Each exit row carries the rank from the last stage actually completed. The field curve counts registrations less all recorded departures through the axis stage. The timing scenarios hold eligibility fixed, vary bonuses and penalties and re-rank the full field at the selected stage. The separate exit budget always uses the actual final winning adjusted time less the rider's completed adjusted time. Divide this by the number of unfinished stages for the required average. For Remco the budget is 31:09:14 across eight stages, averaging 3:53:39. This is a break-even calculation.

**Source and grain.** The importer reconciles individual adjusted times, final GC, final Young Rider and final Best Team against the four CSV views. Relationships run from rider and stage to results, standings and withdrawals, and from team and stage to team aggregates. Country applies the relevant nationality role per measure. The standings checkpoint, classification and scenario selectors are independent dimensions.
